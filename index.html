About IRC

An industrial recycling unit with one vision: a clean and green India.

Batteries, electronics and solar panels are arriving at end of life faster than India can process them safely. IRC is building the plant, the process and the collection network to turn that waste back into raw materials for Indian industry.

Recover

Extract critical minerals to battery grade, so they go back into new products instead of being imported.

Protect

Handle hazardous electrolytes, fluorides and heavy metals safely, with fire-safe processing and treated effluent.

Expand

Start with lithium-ion, then add e-waste, solar panels and other streams on the same platform.

Our process

From scrap pack to battery-grade material.

We are designing IRC to go beyond shredding. Material is taken all the way to refined compounds that cell and cathode makers can use again.

Collect & trace

Reverse logistics from manufacturers, dealers, fleets and aggregators. Every pack is logged for EPR.

→ Graded packs

Test & discharge

Healthy modules go to second-life storage. The rest are safely discharged.

→ Safe cells

Dismantle & shred

Packs are opened and cells shredded in an inert atmosphere to prevent fires.

→ Shred

Separate

Sieving, magnetic and density separation remove steel, aluminium, copper and plastics.

→ Black mass + metals

Leach & purify

Acid leaching, impurity removal and solvent extraction separate each metal.

→ Metal solutions

Refine & return

Crystallised into battery-grade compounds for India's cell supply chain.

→ Li₂CO₃ · NiSO₄ · CoSO₄ · MnSO₄

Research & technology

Why our process is built around hydrometallurgy.

There are three ways to recycle a lithium-ion cell. We chose the route that recovers lithium, runs at low temperature and produces battery-grade output.

Pyrometallurgy

Smelting

Cells are melted in a furnace above 1,400 °C. Cobalt, nickel and copper form an alloy; lithium and aluminium end up in slag.

Recovers
Co, Ni, Cu
Loses
Lithium, graphite, Mn
Trade-off
Simple feed preparation, very high energy use and emissions
Hydrometallurgy

Chemical leaching

Black mass is dissolved in acid at modest temperature. Solvent extraction and precipitation separate each metal into high-purity salts.

Recovers
Li, Ni, Co, Mn (+ Cu, Al, graphite upstream)
Purity
Battery-grade; up to 99.8% Li₂CO₃ reported in published work
Trade-off
Needs careful reagent and effluent management
Direct recycling

Cathode regeneration

The cathode crystal is harvested and re-lithiated without breaking it down into elements. Promising for LFP.

Recovers
Whole cathode material
Status
Pilot stage globally
Trade-off
Needs sorted, single-chemistry feed
Active

Lithium yield

Published studies report lithium recoveries of about 72% to 89% from mixed black mass, with nickel and cobalt above 80%. Our process development targets the top of that range.

Active

LFP economics

LFP cells contain no cobalt or nickel, so value depends on efficient lithium and graphite recovery. We are evaluating direct regeneration for India's large e-rickshaw battery base.

Active

Zero liquid discharge

Closed-loop water and reagent recovery, by-product handling and fluoride capture, so nothing hazardous leaves the site.

Industry & policy

India's battery boom is creating a waste and resource challenge.

Lithium-ion demand is compounding at more than 35% a year. Batteries put into service between 2020 and 2025 start reaching end of life just as IRC comes online.

~800 GWh

Cumulative lithium-ion battery market in India projected by 2030, up from 2.9 GWh in 2018. Every one of those cells eventually retires.

~30%

Yearly growth in India's lithium-ion battery waste. Informal dismantling leaks toxic electrolyte and heavy metals into soil and water and causes fires.

No reserves

India has no meaningful domestic reserves of cobalt or lithium and imports most battery materials. Roughly 30% of a battery's value is in its metals.

Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022

Producers are responsible for recycling

  • Extended Producer Responsibility covers every battery type, including EV batteries. Producers register with CPCB and must meet collection and recycling targets.
  • Registered recyclers generate EPR certificates that producers use to meet their obligations, exchanged on a central online portal.
  • Missed targets attract environmental compensation.

National Critical Mineral Mission

₹1,500 crore recycling incentive

  • 20% capital subsidy on plant, machinery and utilities, plus an operating incentive on incremental sales. Runs FY2025-26 to FY2030-31.
  • Up to ₹50 crore per large entity and ₹25 crore per small entity. One-third of the outlay is reserved for small recyclers and start-ups.
  • Only plants that actually extract critical minerals qualify, not black-mass-only units.
  • Customs duty on lithium-ion battery scrap was removed in FY2025-26.
